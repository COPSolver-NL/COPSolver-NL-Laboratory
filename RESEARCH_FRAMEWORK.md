# RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK

> *"Scientific knowledge is shaped not only by the questions we ask, but also by the principles that guide how we pursue their answers."*

---

# Purpose

The **COPSolver-NL Laboratory** adopts a research framework that guides the production of scientific knowledge across all of its research initiatives.

Rather than prescribing a fixed methodology, this framework establishes enduring principles that promote scientific rigor, conceptual clarity, reproducibility, and continuous refinement.

Individual research projects may employ different mathematical methods, computational techniques, or experimental procedures while remaining grounded in the common scientific principles described in this document.

---

# Research Principles

Scientific research conducted within the laboratory is guided by the following principles:

- Research begins with the understanding of the scientific problem.
- Mathematical formalization provides the foundation for rigorous scientific reasoning.
- Computational methods serve to investigate, validate, and communicate scientific knowledge.
- Scientific conclusions emerge through continuous refinement rather than isolated discoveries.
- Documentation is an integral component of scientific research.
- Transparency and reproducibility strengthen scientific quality.
- Collaboration enriches scientific understanding.

These principles constitute the common scientific foundation shared by all research projects developed within the laboratory.

---

# Knowledge Production

The laboratory understands scientific knowledge as an evolving process.

Research advances through observation, analysis, mathematical formalization, computational experimentation, critical discussion, validation, and continuous refinement.

Knowledge is therefore regarded not as a static result, but as a continuously improving scientific construction.

---

# Scientific Validation

Every scientific contribution should be supported by logical consistency, mathematical rigor, computational verification whenever appropriate, and transparent scientific communication.

Validation is understood as a continuous process accompanying every stage of research rather than a single final step.

---

# Documentation and Reproducibility

Scientific documentation is considered an essential research activity.

Research decisions, mathematical formulations, computational artifacts, experimental procedures, and scientific conclusions should be documented with sufficient clarity to support independent understanding, verification, and future development.

Reproducibility is viewed as a natural consequence of rigorous scientific practice.

---

# Collaboration

The laboratory recognizes collaboration as a fundamental component of scientific progress.

Scientific knowledge benefits from the interaction of researchers with diverse expertise and perspectives.

The laboratory also recognizes the growing contribution of Artificial Intelligence as a collaborative technology capable of supporting scientific exploration, formalization, documentation, and communication.

Human judgment, scientific responsibility, and critical reasoning remain central throughout the research process.

---

# Continuous Evolution

The research framework itself is intended to evolve.

As scientific knowledge advances and new research challenges emerge, this framework may be refined while preserving the principles that define the scientific identity of the laboratory.

Its purpose is not to constrain scientific creativity, but to provide a coherent foundation upon which future research can grow.

---

> *"Methods evolve.*

> *Knowledge grows.*

> *Scientific principles provide continuity."*
