# Welcome

Welcome to the **COPSolver-NL Laboratory**.

This repository documents the scientific development of **COPSolver-NL**, a neuro-symbolic intelligent architecture designed to support the understanding, characterization, and formulation of optimization and decision problems from descriptions of real-world operational processes.

More than a software project, this laboratory is intended to serve as a permanent research environment where ideas, hypotheses, observations, experiments, decisions, and research outcomes are documented as part of the scientific process itself.

The repository preserves both successful developments and approaches that did not produce the expected results, together with the reasoning that motivated them. Both are considered valuable scientific records because they contribute to transparency, reproducibility, and future investigations.

The objective is not only to publish scientific results, but also to preserve the intellectual path through which those results are constructed.

---

# Laboratory Vision

The laboratory is founded on a simple principle:

> Scientific knowledge is not produced only when a result is obtained.

It is produced throughout the entire process of observation, questioning, understanding, modeling, experimentation, revision, and refinement.

For this reason, the laboratory records not only validated scientific results, but also the evolution of ideas that may become future investigations.

> **Research observations should be recorded when they emerge, not when they are already fully understood.**
>
> **Their scientific validation belongs to future investigation.**

---

# Mission

The mission of the COPSolver-NL Laboratory is to investigate intelligent computational methods capable of supporting the understanding of real-world operational processes and their associated challenges, assisting the identification of decision structures and the formulation of optimization and decision models.

The laboratory investigates neuro-symbolic architectures capable of transforming natural descriptions into structured knowledge representations, supporting methodological reasoning during problem formulation while preserving the central role of human expertise.

Rather than reproducing individual cognition, the laboratory investigates methodological structures that can support the scientific process of understanding and formulating optimization and decision problems.

---

# Research Philosophy

The laboratory adopts the following principles.

## 1. Ideas evolve

Initial ideas are expected to be incomplete.

Incomplete ideas are opportunities for future investigation.

Scientific progress emerges through continuous refinement.

---

## 2. Documentation is part of research

Scientific documentation is not viewed as administrative work.

It is itself part of the scientific process.

Recording observations, decisions, assumptions, and research outcomes preserves knowledge that might otherwise be lost.

---

## 3. Scientific honesty precedes certainty

Hypotheses are clearly distinguished from validated scientific knowledge.

Speculation is welcomed when explicitly identified as such.

Scientific validation belongs to future investigation.

---

## 4. The repository reflects the research journey

The repository is intentionally organized to preserve the evolution of scientific reasoning.

Understanding how knowledge was constructed is considered as valuable as presenting the final result.

---

## 5. Scientific responsibility

Every scientific contribution carries consequences.

The laboratory is committed to documenting methods, assumptions, limitations, decisions, and outcomes with transparency, recognizing that scientific knowledge influences future research, technological development, and society.

Responsibility therefore extends beyond obtaining results; it includes preserving the context in which those results were produced.

---

# Scientific Foundations

The scientific foundations of the COPSolver-NL Laboratory are based on the idea that optimization models should emerge from the understanding of real-world operational processes rather than from previously formulated mathematical structures.

The laboratory investigates computational methods capable of transforming descriptions of real-world operational processes and their associated challenges into structured knowledge that supports mathematical modeling.

Two concepts constitute the conceptual foundation of this research.

## Problem-Oriented Knowledge Representation

> **Structured representation of knowledge extracted from the description of real-world operational processes and their associated challenges.**

This representation organizes the knowledge required for understanding the problem domain before mathematical formalization.

---

## Knowledge-Based Assessment

A structured assessment process based on domain knowledge, scientific reasoning, and professional expertise.

Its objective is to analyze the coherence, completeness, consistency, and adequacy of the generated knowledge representation and the proposed mathematical formulation before computational solution.

Knowledge-Based Assessment is understood as a methodological reasoning process rather than an experimental validation procedure.

# Open Science

The laboratory supports the principles of open scientific collaboration.

Whenever possible, documents, software, and scientific discussions are made publicly available to encourage transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative scientific progress.

Open science, however, does not imply unrestricted commercial use.

The licensing adopted by this repository defines the conditions under which its contents may be reused.

# Founding Statement

Every research project begins with an idea.

Every idea begins incomplete.

Scientific progress begins when ideas become explicit, discussable, and methodologically structured.

The purpose of this laboratory is not merely to produce optimization models.

Its purpose is to investigate methods capable of supporting the understanding of real-world operational processes, identifying decision structures, and assisting the formulation of optimization and decision models.

In doing so, the laboratory seeks to contribute not only to intelligent optimization systems, but also to the methodological foundations through which scientific knowledge in this field is constructed.
